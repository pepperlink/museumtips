Den Haag

Kunstmuseum Den Haag is a modern palace for the arts in a magnificent Berlage building. It houses a leading collection of modern and contemporary visual arts, fashion and applied arts, in one of Europe's largest art museums. The museum is home to Piet Mondriaan, with as many as 300 of his artworks.

Getting there

Public transport

Tram 17 and bus 24 (HTM) from The Hague Central Station. Tram stop approx. 50 m from the main entrance; bus 24 stop approx. 130 m. Tram 17 and bus 24 have disabled access; mobility scooters and electric wheelchairs cannot be taken on HTM buses.

Parking

Paid parking in front of the museum from 1 pm, per the EN page € 2.50 per hour (NL page states € 2.75). Two disabled parking spaces at the main entrance (free with a disabled parking card); if occupied, only street parking remains. No indoor car parks with adapted spaces in the vicinity.

Accessibility

The museum aims to welcome everyone, but the Berlage building does not have good disabled access throughout. Wheelchairs, rollators and the Rollz Motion can be borrowed at the information desk; advance reservation advised via 070 - 338 12 19. Assistants accompanying visitors receive a free ticket. Two adapted toilets (ground floor and level -1); all floors accessible by lift (central lift 107 x 155 cm). Some galleries have heavy doors and raised thresholds (up to 4 cm); staff are happy to help. Assistance dogs are welcome. Mobile induction loops are available in the auditorium; generally quieter times are 15.00 - 17.00.
