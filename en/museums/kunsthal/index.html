Rotterdam

Kunsthal Rotterdam is an exhibition building on the edge of the Museumpark, designed by Rem Koolhaas (OMA) and officially opened on 1 November 1992; it is considered an icon of modern architecture. The Kunsthal is not a museum and, with no collection of its own, programmes around 25 changing exhibitions a year — often several at once — at the intersection of art, culture and society. The building has seven exhibition halls, an auditorium and a café.

Getting there

Public transport

From Rotterdam Central Station: tram 8 towards Spangen, stop Kievitslaan (1 minute walk); tram 4 or 6 towards Heemraadsplein, stop Eendrachtsplein (7 minutes); metro Eendrachtsplein or Leuvehaven (7 minutes) or Dijkzigt (10 minutes); about a 20-minute walk from Rotterdam Central. Note: RET works may mean adjusted tram routes — check the RET journey planner.

Parking

Museumpark parking garage (sat nav: Museumpark 32, 3015 CX Rotterdam; accessible only from Wytemaweg); capacity is limited due to construction work — book early. Alternative: Apcoa Parking in the Scheepvaartkwartier (6-minute walk). Disabled parking spaces on the service road of the Westzeedijk (disabled parking permit required). Bicycles: unsupervised racks at the entrance, at the bottom of the slope.

Accessibility

All exhibition spaces except the sloped ramp in HAL 6 are wheelchair accessible, and all corridors are wide enough; staff assist where needed. Wheelchairs can be borrowed free of charge (reserve in advance via 010-4400 300 or receptie@kunsthal.nl). Mobility scooters are allowed if the size of an electric wheelchair. Lightweight folding stools are available at the start and end of HAL 1 and 2. Assistance dogs are welcome. Visitors unable to visit independently may bring a companion free of charge (book a free companion ticket online). The Kunsthal is cashless (card only).
