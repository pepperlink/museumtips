Groningen

Groninger Museum is the largest museum in the north of the Netherlands for art and design, home to over 60,000 objects and artworks spanning five millennia. The museum building on the Museumeiland is a postmodern design by, among others, Alessandro Mendini, Philippe Starck, Michele De Lucchi and Coop Himmelb(l)au. Its collection, displays and changing exhibitions focus on timely topics through the lens of contemporary art and design.

Opening hours

Tue–Sun 10 am–5 pm. Closed on Mondays. Extra closures: Friday 18 September 2026, 25 December (Christmas Day) and 1 January (New Year's Day).

Getting there

Public transport

Train: the museum is about 300 metres from Groningen Central Station. No bus information is given on the visit page.

Parking

Q-Park garages in the city centre (nearest: Q-Park Museum Centrum) or P+R sites on the edge of the city. Disabled parking: two spaces on the Ubbo Emmiussingel next to the museum. Bicycle: racks on the Ubbo Emmiussingel or the Stadsbalkon storage at Central Station. Note: zero-emission zone since 1 April 2025.

Accessibility

Easily accessible for wheelchairs and mobility scooters; a few wheelchairs, walking frames and a folding stool can be borrowed (reservation recommended: +31 50 366 6555 / info@groningermuseum.nl). Accessible toilet on level -1; free companion tickets via the ticket page; assistance dogs welcome. Low-stimulus openings, sign-language tours and tours for blind and partially sighted visitors. Colour-blindness correction glasses available free of charge at the information desk. Museum Shuttle: pick-up and drop-off on request.
