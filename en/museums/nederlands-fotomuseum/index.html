Nederlands Fotomuseum

Back to all museums

Rotterdam

The Nederlands Fotomuseum (Dutch National Museum of Photography) is housed in the monumental Santos warehouse at Rotterdam's Rijnhaven, in the Katendrecht district. The museum manages and opens up Dutch photographic heritage — from the invention of photography around 1839 to present-day makers — and is a global leader in collection management; the collection is expected to reach around 7.5 million objects by 2028. Displays include the Gallery of Honour of Dutch Photography and open depots and studios.

Opening hours

Tuesday to Sunday 11.00–17.00; closed Mondays. The museum is open on Easter Sunday and Monday, Liberation Day, Ascension Day, Whit Sunday and Monday, and Boxing Day. Please book a time slot online; have your e-ticket and discount card ready at the entrance.

Getting there

Public transport

Rijnhaven metro station is about a 2-minute walk from the museum — you can see the building from the platform. The station has an elevator.

Parking

Q-Park Rijnhaven car park is a short walk away (Pudongstraat 4, Rotterdam) and has wheelchair-accessible parking spaces and lifts. The nearest disabled parking space is opposite the museum on the Bananenstraat (European disabled parking permit required). Bicycle racks are available directly in front of the building.

Accessibility

The Nederlands Fotomuseum is fully wheelchair accessible; the entrance is reached via a signposted ramp. There are lifts and wheelchair-accessible toilets (basement and sixth floor), automatic doors and easy-to-reach lift buttons. A wheelchair can be borrowed at the reception desk (advance reservation recommended via info@nederlandsfotomuseum.nl or +31 (0)10 203 04 05). Assistance, service and guide dogs are warmly welcomed. Seating and rest areas are available; four lockers for wheelchair users are located on the ground floor.
