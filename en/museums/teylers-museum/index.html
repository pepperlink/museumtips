Haarlem

Teylers Museum in Haarlem is the oldest museum in the Netherlands. It originated from the ideals of Pieter Teyler van der Hulst (1702-1778); the Oval Room opened to the public in 1784. In its historic building, art and science meet; the collections include drawings, paintings, scientific instruments, fossils and books. Curiosity and critical thought are central to the museum's identity.

Getting there

Public transport

The museum is easily accessible by public transport and within walking distance (about 13 minutes) of Haarlem train station. By bus from Haarlem Centraal: line 3 (direction Schalkwijk) to the Turfmarkt stop, the nearest stop; then about a 6-minute walk.

Parking

Parking is limited in the centre of Haarlem; consider using public transport or nearby parking garages: - Parking garage De Appelaar: 170 m from the museum entrance - Parking garage De Kamp: 500 m - Parking garage Raaks: 900 m

Accessibility

Teylers Museum is wheelchair and walker accessible; all doors are at least 85 cm wide. A few historical rooms are not easily accessible. Two wheelchair-accessible toilets. Assistance dogs are welcome (on a lead). Mobility scooters are not permitted due to the vulnerable set-up; electric wheelchairs are. Two wheelchairs available (reserve via info@teylersmuseum.nl). Wheelchair entrance: Spaarne 18 (doorbell/intercom; staff will open the door). Main entrance Spaarne 16 has three steps. Free admission for assistants: 'Assistant' ticket at the desk or online. Stools and a free audio guide (player) available at the desk. The first floor (lecture room, library) is accessible only as part of a guided tour and less easily accessible; notify in advance. The Pieter Teyler House is accessible via the original front door at Damstraat 21 (ground floor only; no lift).
