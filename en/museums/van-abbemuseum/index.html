Eindhoven

The Van Abbemuseum in Eindhoven is one of the first public museums for contemporary art to be established in Europe. Its collection of over 3,600 works of art includes key works by artists such as Joseph Beuys, Marc Chagall, René Daniëls, Marlene Dumas, El Lissitzky, Pablo Picasso and Lidwien van de Ven. The museum has an experimental approach towards art's role in society; openness, hospitality and knowledge exchange are important to it.

Getting there

Public transport

From Eindhoven Central Station it is a 15-minute walk through the car-free city centre to the museum. Every half hour, trains from Amsterdam, Utrecht, The Hague, Rotterdam, Venlo, Heerlen and Maastricht arrive in Eindhoven (check ns.nl). By bus: the bus station is on the north side of the station; buses stopping near the museum include stops Bilderdijklaan (1-minute walk), Smalle Haven (3 minutes), Stadhuisplein (4 minutes), PC Hooftlaan (4 minutes) and Keizersgracht (9 minutes). Check 9292.nl for the latest travel information.

Parking

Parking garages within walking distance: Q-Park Stadhuisplein and Parking Stadskantoor (5-minute walk) and Q-Park Heuvel (10-minute walk). Travelling from the south? Park inexpensively at P+R Genneperparken and continue by bus, shared bicycle or scooter. Two disabled parking spaces are located next to the main entrance at Stratumsedijk 2 (assistance: 040 238 10 20). Bicycles: parking next to the main entrance; guarded parking at Fietsenstalling Heuvel, a 10-minute walk away; the museum is connected to bicycle junction 89.

Accessibility

The Van Abbemuseum is well accessible with wheelchairs, mobility scooters and walkers. The museum has two wheelchairs for visitor use; you can reserve them by sending an email to kassabalie@vanabbemuseum.nl. Guide dogs (on a leash) are allowed; other dogs are not. A limited number of free lockers are available for storing coats, bags larger than A3 size, backpacks and umbrellas.
