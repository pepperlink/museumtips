De Pont Museum

Back to all museums

Tilburg

De Pont Museum is an international museum for contemporary art, housed in a former wool mill in Tilburg. Benthem Crouwel Architects transformed the industrial building with its distinctive sawtooth roof into an open, light-filled space. Since opening in 1992, the museum has changed its exhibitions and collection displays several times a year; painting and sculpture, along with installations, film, performance and photography, form the heart of the programme. In 2016 the museum opened a new wing for photography, video and multimedia installations, which also houses the museum café. The collection holds over a thousand works by more than a hundred artists; the museum is named after Jan de Pont (1915-1987).

Getting there

Public transport

The museum is approximately 15 minutes on foot from Tilburg Central Station (exit on the north side, turn left onto Burgemeester Brokxlaan, turn right at Gasthuisring, then left just past the Wilhelminapark). Buses from Tilburg station: bus 5 towards Noord-Stokhasselt, stop Textielmuseum; bus 7 towards West ETZ Tweesteden, stop Kwaadeindstraat. For up-to-date train and bus times, visit 9292.nl.

Parking

De Pont Museum has its own car park with space for 20 vehicles; a parking ticket costs € 5 (pay by card at the exit terminal or with an exit ticket available from the museum reception during opening hours). Paid parking is in operation in the surrounding area. Car parks within walking distance (less than 10 minutes): Knegtel and Spoorzone-Zwijsen. Two spaces for visitors with disabilities are located next to the museum's own car park (cannot be reserved).

Accessibility

Mobility aids including wheelchairs, walking frames and small mobility scooters are welcome in the museum. A number of wheelchairs and walking frames, plus folding stools, can be borrowed free of charge from the reception desk; please notify the museum in advance by email so arrangements can be made. The single-storey museum is generously laid out; as a historic former factory, not every room offers the same level of access. Two collection works have limited accessibility for visitors with disabilities: Wachsraum by Wolfgang Laib and Wedgework III by James Turrell. Visitors who are unable to move through the museum independently are welcome to bring a carer or companion free of charge. The museum has two accessible toilets. Assistance dogs are welcome: KNGF dogs with an identification card; for assistance dogs from other organisations, please contact the museum in advance. Two parking spaces for blue badge holders are on municipal land approximately 100 metres from the main entrance (cannot be reserved).
