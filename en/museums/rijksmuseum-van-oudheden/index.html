Leiden

The Dutch National Museum of Antiquities (RMO) in Leiden houses a collection of some 180,000 objects across four areas: Egypt and Nubia, Classical Antiquity, the Ancient Near East, and the Netherlands (prehistory, Roman period, and the Middle Ages). The museum was founded in 1818 as Leiden University's archaeological cabinet and celebrated its 200th anniversary in 2018. Since 1995 it has been an independent non-profit organisation managing the archaeological part of the Dutch national collection. A highlight is the 2,000-year-old Egyptian temple from Taffeh, on view in the entrance hall.

Getting there

Public transport

The museum is located in Leiden's historic city centre, about a 10-minute walk from Leiden Central railway station.

Parking

There are no parking spaces available near the museum; the museum advises parking on the Haagweg site or in one of the parking garages. One disabled parking space is available in front of the museum on the Rapenburg (an official disabled parking permit is required).

Accessibility

The entire museum is accessible to wheelchair users and visually impaired visitors and is part of the Disabilities Sunflower network. All floors are connected by lifts. Wheelchairs can be borrowed at the reception desk (€ 2 refundable deposit; users must be accompanied). Guide dogs are welcome. For visually impaired visitors there is a guided tour and workshop about ancient Egypt (free with museum admission). Free Wi-Fi is available; photography is allowed (no flash or tripods). Only small handbags may be taken in (backpacks are not allowed); lockers are available.
